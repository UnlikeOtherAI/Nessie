# Licensing

## Plain English summary

- **Self-hosting is free**, including for commercial and internal business
  use by companies of any size. Run Nessie for your own organisation, teams,
  and employees without paying for a license or asking permission.
- **You can read, modify, and fork the code** for your own use, for
  non-commercial education or research, or while providing professional
  services (integration, support, customisation) to someone else running
  Nessie.
- **You cannot take a current release and turn it into a competing
  commercial product or service** — for example, hosting it as a paid
  multi-tenant SaaS that substitutes for Nessie itself, or for any other
  product UnlikeOtherAI offers using this code.
- **Every release converts to Apache License 2.0 automatically, two years
  after its publication date.** At that point the competing-use restriction
  disappears for that release, and it becomes permissively licensed open
  source. UnlikeOtherAI keeps shipping newer releases under FSL, so the
  currently shipping product stays protected while the two-year-old code
  becomes fully open.

This is the [Functional Source License, Version 1.1, Apache 2.0 Future
License](https://fsl.software/) (FSL-1.1-ALv2). The full legal terms are in
[`../LICENSE`](../LICENSE); this page is a summary, not a substitute for
reading it.

## Why FSL

Nessie needs to be safely self-hostable by any organisation — including
competitors' internal teams — without becoming free infrastructure for a
competing hosted Nessie product built on today's code. FSL-1.1-ALv2 gives
both: unrestricted self-hosting and internal use today, and a firm two-year
commitment that the code becomes genuinely open (Apache-2.0), release by
release.

## Contributions

Contributions to this repository are licensed under the same terms as the
rest of the codebase: FSL-1.1-ALv2, on the same two-year path to Apache
License 2.0. If you are contributing on behalf of an organisation other than
UnlikeOtherAI, or the contribution is substantial, UnlikeOtherAI may ask you
to sign a separate contributor agreement before merging it.
